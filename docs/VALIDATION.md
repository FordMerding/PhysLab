## 3. Dynamometer (force = impulse per step / step length)

The force readout of a body is the sum of all joint and contact impulses applied to it during one 1/120 s step (all four substeps, warm-start included, position correction excluded) divided by 1/120 s.
Summed over time it reproduces Newton's second law to machine precision (`impulse balance` tests, difference < 1e-9 N s). A single step is noisy for light ropes, so use the time average or the smoothed value (`smoothForceX/Y`).

## 4. Known limitation: block and tackle with a light rope

Movable pulley with load M, handle mass mh, rope over a fixed pulley (two rope segments carry the movable pulley).
Ideal theory: a(handle) = (mh - M/2) g / (mh + M/4). Acceleration is measured between 0.5 s and 0.9 s.
These rows are **not** pass/fail tests: they show where the chain-of-nodes rope is not accurate. Do not use quantitative readouts from this regime.
For textbook pulley problems use the ideal rope (the Pulley rope tool, `PulleyRope`, or the scenes Atwood machine and Block and tackle with an ideal rope): it has no mass and no stretch, and the ideal-rope and Pulley-rope rows above reproduce the formulas to better than 1 %.
